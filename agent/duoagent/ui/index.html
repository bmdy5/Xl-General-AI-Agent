10:42 AM Elias Thorne

If an autonomous system makes a decision that results in harm, but followed its utility function perfectly, where does the moral culpability reside? Is it the designer, the dataset, or the system itself?

Citation Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics
menu_book
Sage 10:45 AM

To address this, we must look to classical conceptions of agency. Aristotle argued that an action is voluntary—and thus subject to moral evaluation—only if the 'moving principle' is within the agent, and the agent is aware of the particular circumstances of the action.

An autonomous system possesses a 'moving principle' (its algorithms), but does it possess awareness? Without true comprehension of consequences, it acts as a tool rather than a moral agent. Therefore, culpability logically traces back to the prime movers: the designers and the data curators.
